# Worked example: the Hegseth impeachment vote (November 10, 2026)

A real concern, run through the workflow in SKILL.md, for a user in Buda, Texas whose saved officials file was already set up and fresh.

## The user said

"11 Democrats recently didn't show up to impeach Pete Hegseth. I'm upset about it."

## Step 1: Verify on the official record before anything else

The workflow requires checking the claim against official sources, not repeating it. The official record (House Clerk, Roll Call 317, and H.Res. 1655 on congress.gov) showed:

- The House did not vote on impeachment itself. On November 10, 2026 it voted on a motion to refer Rep. Thomas Massie's eight articles of impeachment against Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth (H.Res. 1655) to the Judiciary Committee.
- The motion passed 211 to 204. Eighteen members did not vote: 11 Democrats and 7 Republicans. The 203 Democrats who voted all voted against referral.
- With a 7 vote margin, the absences plausibly changed the outcome. The user's frustration had a factual basis, with one correction: a referral vote, not an impeachment vote.

## Step 2: Choose the level

Impeachment of a cabinet secretary is federal, and at this stage House only. The Senate's role (a trial) exists only if the House impeaches. State officials have no role at all. Nobody in Austin gets a message.

## Step 3: Fast path

The user's officials file was verified that day, so no new lookup. Current U.S. Representative: Chip Roy (TX-21).

## Step 4: Committee check

H.Res. 1655 now sits in the House Judiciary Committee. The user's representative sits on the Judiciary Committee, which makes him a primary contact: he can act on the articles in committee.

## Step 5: Non-representative check

The 11 absent Democrats are not the user's representatives. Their official contact forms gate by ZIP+4 constituency and would reject or ignore a submission from this user. The workflow does not waste submissions on them. Instead it checks the official roll call for the user's own representative: he voted Yea on the motion to refer.

## Result: exactly one contact

**Chip Roy**, for three reasons that compound: he is the user's representative, he personally voted to refer the articles, and he sits on the committee that now holds them. The message is aimed at his own record and his committee seat, not at the absent Democrats.

Not contacted: the two U.S. Senators (premature; the Senate acts only after a House impeachment), the state senator, state representative, and governor (no state role), and the absent members (not his representatives).

## Step 6: The authorization gate

"I'm upset about it" states a feeling, not an ask, so the draft first exception applies even under a standing authorization: the assistant drafts the message to the representative and shows it to the user before submitting. Once the user states the ask (for example, "demand he push the articles in Judiciary"), the same instruction authorizes submission, and the assistant reports back the exact text and the confirmation.
