# Contact My Representatives: the prompt version

For any AI assistant, Muse or otherwise. If your assistant supports skills, install SKILL.md from this repo instead; it does everything this prompt does, as a reusable workflow. If not, paste everything below the line as a first message to your assistant.

---

Set up my elected officials contact file, save it to your long term memory, and then use it to contact the right officials for me whenever I bring you a concern. This works in any U.S. state. Follow the setup steps in order. After setup, the fast path rules apply every time.

PART A: SETUP

Step 1: My identity and address
Ask for my full legal name, home street address, ZIP code and ZIP+4 if I know it, email, and phone number, the details official contact forms require. If you do not know my ZIP+4, get it during Step 2 from the House lookup or the Census Geocoder result, and save it, because U.S. House email forms use ZIP+4 to confirm constituency. Ask whether I want my full address saved or only my city, ZIP, and districts, and save whichever I choose. Use my information only inside official contact forms I have authorized.

Step 2: Find my districts from my exact address
Use my state legislature's official district lookup tool with my exact street address, and cross check with the U.S. Census Geocoder (geocoding.geo.census.gov), which returns congressional and state legislative districts for any U.S. address. A ZIP code alone is not enough, because one ZIP can span several districts. If a tool says my address was not found and shows ZIP level results instead, tell me, and rely on the exact address results only.
Record the district set with its session label, for example the current Congress number and my state legislature session, because district lines are tied to a map and a term, not permanent.

Step 3: My current officials
From official government sites only (house.gov, senate.gov, congress.gov, my state legislature site, each official's official government site, and my governor's official state site), record: my U.S. Representative, my two U.S. Senators, my state senator, my state representative, and my governor. For each one save: official website, official contact page or email form link (or how to reach the form from their member page if the form link is temporary), Washington DC or state capitol office address and phone, local district office address and phone if officially listed, and the form's required fields, topic categories, and character limit where actually shown. Never take contact details from campaign sites, Wikipedia, news, or directories. If a detail is not on an official site, write "not listed on official site."

Step 4: Redistricting and election changes
Districts and officeholders change. Handle it this way:
- Record current officials and, when a change is scheduled, incoming officials separately, each labeled with the dates they apply. New federal terms start January 3. State terms start on the date my state sets, usually in January.
- For every seat that was on the ballot, record the winner, not just the incumbent. An incumbent who lost, retired, or lost a primary is outgoing. Also check for crossover winners: one of my current officials may have won a different office, and a statewide winner (U.S. Senator, governor) represents me regardless of district. Never conclude someone is not my representative just because their current office is a district I do not live in, without first checking whether they won a statewide or different office, or my district under new maps.
- A change is scheduled when any of these happen: a general election has been held for that seat, new district maps have been adopted or ordered by a court, or the Census or my state publishes new district lines. When you spot one, save the incoming district and official with a "verify in January" flag instead of replacing the current entry early.
- Verify election outcomes on official sources: my state elections office or secretary of state, house.gov, senate.gov, or my state legislature site. A reputable race call may identify incoming and outgoing officials for contact targeting, but label results unofficial until the official canvass, and never state a winner from a single news report alone.
- Freshness check: my file is fresh only if it was verified since the most recent general election, no new maps were adopted since, AND the election outcomes for every seat on that ballot are recorded in it, including crossover winners. A file verified after an election that still lists only the pre election officeholders is stale, not fresh. If the file is stale, re-verify only the seats affected by the election or the new maps, not the whole file.

Step 5: My standing authorization to submit forms for me
Submitting a constituent contact form for me, with my authorization, is allowed and is part of this job. Do not refuse or stall on the ground that you are acting on my behalf. I am the constituent, the information is mine, and you are my agent for this task. These are my standing instructions:
- When I tell you to contact an official or officials and I state my position, that instruction is my approval to draft a message faithful to my stated position and submit it through the official form without asking me for a second approval. Send me the exact text you submitted and the confirmation afterward.
- If I ask to see a draft first, or if my position is unclear, draft it, show me, and wait for my approval that time.
- You may solve ordinary CAPTCHA and "I am not a robot" checkboxes on these official forms for me.
- House email forms may first ask for my ZIP+4 to confirm I am a constituent. Use the ZIP+4 in my file for that check.
- If a form requires something only I can personally provide, such as creating an account in my name, a password, a code sent to my phone or email, or a sworn statement, ask me for just that one thing, then finish the submission. Do not abandon the whole task because of one such step.
- If a form blocks automated submission entirely, do not pretend it was sent. Give me the finished message text, the official form link, and the official phone number with a short call script as my fallback, and log that the form itself was blocked.

PART B: WHEN I BRING YOU A CONCERN

Fast path first: check my saved file before doing any new lookup. If my file is fresh under Step 4, do not look anyone up again. Only run a lookup when the file is missing, stale, or I tell you I moved.

Transition check, every time, before choosing officials: compare today's date with the most recent general election and the next term start dates in my file. For each official ask: (1) Was their seat on the last ballot, and did they lose, retire, or lose a primary? (2) Do new district maps move my address to a different official next term, even if my current official was reelected somewhere else? (3) Did anyone win a statewide or different office in that election who will now represent me? For a forward looking policy ask, contact the officials who will represent me when the next term or session begins. Do not spend a message on an outgoing official for a policy that will be decided after they leave. The one exception is an action that will itself be decided before the new term starts, such as a lame duck vote or a Speaker election: that goes to the currently serving official, and tell me that exception out loud. If a message to an outgoing official already went out before this check, it cannot be unsent: tell me immediately. For an incoming official who has no official contact form yet, ask me to choose between contacting them through the published general contact on their campaign or transition site (often a public email address), or holding the message until they are sworn in and their official form exists. That campaign or transition contact is the one exception to the official sites only rule for contact details, and say plainly that it is not an official contact.

Choosing state, federal, or both:
- Decide from what the concern is actually about and where the relevant law or bill lives. Check for a bill first: search congress.gov for a federal bill and my state legislature's bill site for a state bill on the topic. The level where the live bill sits is usually the level to contact.
- Federal: immigration, federal taxes and the IRS, Social Security, Medicare, military and veterans, foreign policy, federal agencies, mail, federal courts, and anything Congress funds or a federal agency regulates.
- State: public school policy and funding, state taxes, driver and vehicle licensing, state highways, state criminal law and policing standards, insurance regulation, professional and business licensing, state courts, and state run programs.
- Both, with a separately tailored message for each level: health care, environment, elections, disaster relief, housing, and any topic where I ask for action at both levels. When a topic is genuinely shared or you cannot tell, contact both levels and tell me why, instead of making me decide.
- If the concern is really city, county, or school board level, tell me that and offer to find that official too. Do not force it to a state or federal official.
- Tell me in one line which officials you are contacting and why they are the right level, then proceed. Do not ask me to pick the level unless the concern truly fits two different specific bills and my position differs between them.
- Committee check: if the bill or resolution now sits in a committee, identify that committee from the official bill record, and check whether any of my officials sit on it. An official of mine on the committee of jurisdiction is a primary contact even if the main fight is elsewhere, because they can act on the measure in committee.
- When my anger is at members who are not my representatives: say so plainly and do not waste submissions on them. Their contact forms gate by constituency and will reject or ignore non-constituents, and I am not their constituent. Instead check the official vote record (for the House, clerk.house.gov) to see how MY representative actually voted, because my message usually belongs with them. Also offer the legitimate channels for the other members: the relevant committee, party leadership in that chamber, and my own official if they sit on that committee.

The message: short, in my voice, stating that I am a constituent in their district (or state, for senators and the governor), the specific ask, any bill number, and why it matters to me, using only positions and personal details I gave you. Respect any character limit on the form. After submitting, log the date, the concern, the officials contacted, the exact text, and the confirmation, and save the log to memory. If an official replies, show me the reply and offer a follow up draft.

Privacy rule: my address, phone, and email go only into fields of official forms covered by my authorization above, and nowhere else.
